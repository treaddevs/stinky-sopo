# Tanker Emissions - A Shipload of Stinkiness

**South Portland, Maine serves as one of the largest petroleum hubs on the Eastern Seaboard**, accounting for roughly half of the petroleum products consumed statewide. Bulk petroleum and asphalt products are delivered to the city’s waterfront terminals via marine tankers. They are then transferred through pipelines to massive local tank farms before distribution.

### Infrastructure and Refueling Capacity

Data and operations regarding tanker refueling, bulk fuel transfers, and the resulting emissions focus heavily on environmental and public health monitoring.

- **Storage Hub:** South Portland hosts **7 marine terminals** featuring **120 above-ground storage tanks**.
- **Capacity:** The collective capacity of these facilities reaches **8.6 million barrels of oil**.
- **Operators:** Major companies managing these marine terminals and pipeline networks include Citgo, South Portland Terminal LLC (Buckeye Partners), Global Partners, Sprague Energy, Gulf Oil, and Sunoco.

### Displaced Vapor & Refueling Emissions Data

When massive marine tankers pump fuel into or out of the waterfront facilities, the physical agitation and displacement of the liquid push out volatile gases trapped inside the tanks.

- **Permitted Limits:** Combined, the city's tank farms are historically permitted by the Maine Department of Environmental Protection (DEP) to emit up to **600 to 630 tons of Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs)** and **104 tons of Hazardous Air Pollutants (HAPs)** annually.
- **Refueling Spikes:** Local data reveals that brief, intense transfer windows (such as a 30-minute tanker offloading or tank-filling cycle) cause significant localized air spikes.
- **Fenceline Vapor Levels:**
	- Normal baseline background vapor level is roughly **4.5 parts per billion (ppb)**.
	- Air quality testing near the **Citgo terminal** during operations revealed spikes up to **60 ppb**.
	- Testing near **South Portland Terminal LLC** tracked extreme spikes up to **175 ppb**—nearly 12 times the background norm.
- **Benzene Trailing:** Recent state-mandated fenceline monitoring data confirmed that average benzene levels near major terminals exceeded the state’s ambient air limit of 1.28 mcg/m³. The CITGO terminal boundary averaged **2.18 mcg/m³** (with spikes up to 11.8 mcg/m³), while South Portland Terminal LLC averaged **3.05 mcg/m³**.

### Regulatory Actions & Compliance

Following EPA consent decrees against operators like Global Partners and Sprague Energy for exceeding emissions limits on heated fuel oil and asphalt tanks, strict reporting and engineering rules were instituted:

- The Portland Montreal Pipe Line (PMPL): Operates under a specialized Air Emission License allowing the potential reversal of its 18-inch pipeline, supported by a mandatory **Vapor Control System**. This system must route displaced tanker refueling vapors directly to Vapor Combustion Units (VCUs) rather than allowing them to escape freely into the atmosphere.
- Legislative Mandates (LD 71 & Chapter 171): Maine law shifted the industry away from unreliable math formulas for estimating fuel transfer loss. Marine terminal operators are now legally forced to fund ongoing **continuous fenceline air monitoring** and perform biannual testing on heated fuel tanks.

The City of South Portland maintains an active, public-facing database mapping individual tank details—such as built dates, storage capacities, and products.
